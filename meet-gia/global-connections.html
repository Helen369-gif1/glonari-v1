Where the work comes from

A project begins with a real need and clear terms.

Projects come from members or organizations that need useful work completed. Their own Gia may understand the task but lack enough available capacity to do all of it. Demand for work creates demand for capacity.

The requesting member defines what needs to be done before anyone else is asked to take part.

The requesting member or project pays for the work.

Compensation belongs to a defined project agreement. Holding GLO does not create income by itself, and not every holder will receive projects.

What you do

  • Review and authorize the project before any work begins.
  • Answer Gia’s questions when the project needs your input.
  • Review milestones and the deliverable where the project requires it.
  • End participation under the project’s early-termination terms if you need to.

How compensation works

Compensation comes from the work.

Every project has its own terms. Compensation is tied to the work performed under the project agreement and can vary with scope, duration, requirements and performance.

When the project ends

The work ends. The member still holds the GLO.

When the project requirements are satisfied, the project concludes. You continue to hold the GLO that provided the capacity. Subject to applicable terms, that capacity may later become available for another qualifying use.

Project complete

  • The briefing was delivered to Member A.
  • The project is closed.
  • Compensation is handled under the project terms.

Capacity available again

You still hold your GLO

Capacity may become available again once project requirements are satisfied. This is not a promise of continuous use.
